# VVU EARTH TECH — Governance Principles

## Execution Principle

VVU pursues multiple independent resource acquisition pathways in parallel, including strategic partnerships, research collaborations, public funding, private investment, sponsorship, and community support. This diversified approach is intended to reduce reliance on any single organisation while maintaining disciplined programme governance. All collaboration activities remain subject to formal review, approval, and execution.

## Communications Policy

VVU follows a staged disclosure approach. Public communications focus on verified engineering progress and approved information, while sensitive technical details, intellectual property, security-related information, and ongoing partnership discussions are disclosed only on a need-to-know basis and under appropriate confidentiality arrangements where applicable.

## Status Classification

All resource acquisition activities are classified using a three-tier status system:

| Status | Definition |
|--------|-----------|
| **Strategy** | Planned direction and objectives — not yet active |
| **Active Outreach** | Currently being pursued, no outcomes secured yet |
| **Confirmed Commitment** | Formal agreement executed and verified |

This distinction is structural, not aspirational. Presenting pathways as planned and active initiatives — rather than as outcomes that have already been secured — makes the document stronger, not weaker.

## Pilot Municipality

**City of Cape Town** — Active outreach, no executed agreements.

The Nelson Mandela Bay Metro (NMBM) was originally considered as the pilot case study but has been replaced due to governance instability and irregular use of municipal funds over the past year. Cape Town provides a more operationally stable environment for the 72-hour resilience validation.
