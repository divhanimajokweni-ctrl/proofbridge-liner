  constructor(lat,lon,alt=0){this.o=g2e(lat,lon,alt);
    const la=lat*DEG,lo=lon*DEG;this.sl=Math.sin(la);this.cl=Math.cos(la);

# 1️⃣ Related-Work Section (manuscript-ready)

## II. Related Work

The transition of distribution networks toward inverter-dominated operation compresses three engineering problems into one design space: the grid must absorb generation whose behavior is dictated by compliance standards, the conversion assets mediating that exchange degrade in ways that are increasingly hard to observe, and the communication fabric binding them must remain cheap, reliable, and standard-conformant. The works surveyed here attack these problems at separate layers of the same stack; their combination exposes the gap this thesis/manuscript addresses.

### A. Grid Integration of Inverter-Interfaced Generation

Compliance with IEEE 1547 volt-var control extracts a measurable energy price from distributed photovoltaic (PV) owners, and Issa *et al.* [1] provide the most complete quantification of that price to date. Integrating IEEE 1547 compliance, feeder loading and strength, distributed energy resource (DER) penetration, and NSRDB-derived irradiance models [9] into full-year OpenDSS–MATLAB co-simulations over three IEEE test feeders [8], they show that for any feeder there exists an optimal penetration percentage $S_P$ (25–35% across the 13-, 34-, and 123-bus cases) minimizing relative curtailment. Below this optimum, lost generation is attributable to the feeder's *own* undervoltage regulation burden rather than the PVs; above it, reverse power flow dominates. The asymmetry is feeder-dependent: weak feeders suffer at both penetration extremes, strong feeders only at low penetration. Two second-order findings matter for planners: fixed capacitor banks asymmetrically benefit low-penetration operation, and on-load tap changers, while globally beneficial, can *increase* the volt-var effort demanded of inverters outside their effective regulation sections. Earlier studies had evaluated mixed constant-power-factor/voltage-active modes [1, refs. 18–19] or compared support modes on low-voltage feeders, where reactive algorithms are inherently less effective [1, ref. 20]; [1] extends the analysis to medium voltage with real irradiance and a hosting-capacity (HC) interpretation: HC as a function of the curtailment level tolerable by consumer-owned DERs.

Where [1] accepts converter coupling and prices its side effects, Gerber and Kamper [5] remove the converter entirely. Their slip-synchronous wind turbine system (SS-WTS) connects a dual-speed synchronous generator directly to the grid, with damping provided by a slip permanent-magnet coupling (S-PMC). Their central contribution is a four-parameter stability map — S-PMC rated slip, turbine-to-generator inertia ratio κ, generator synchronous reactance $X_s$, and S-PMC pullout torque — establishing that under the most severe symmetrical three-phase low-voltage ride-through (LVRT), synchronous reactance must fall to 0.29 p.u. for the geared topology, that the folk rule "pullout torque > 2 p.u." is not universal (required values span 1.4–2.2 p.u. depending on slip and κ), and that the high-speed S-PMC placement dominates on mass, cost, and damping. Notably, the paper corrects prior stability claims in the SS-WTS literature: modeling the S-PMC as a simple gain produces incorrect LVRT results, and prior stability demonstrations at 50% load do not extend to rated operation. Both [1] and [5] thus convert standards compliance into *design-space constraints* — tolerable curtailment in one case, admissible reactance and pullout torque in the other — a symmetry this work exploits.

### B. Condition Monitoring of Conversion Assets

Two complementary failure modes motivate drive-level diagnostics: insulation degradation and winding faults. Lee *et al.* [2] review partial discharge (PD) in wide-bandgap (WBG) drives, where high slew rates invalidate the classical assumption that peak turn voltage — and hence PD location — occurs at the first turn. Using high-frequency π-models across capacitive, inductive, and distributed PD-object geometries (with and without rotor), they show that LC resonance can migrate the peak-voltage location beyond the first turn, that skin and surface/space charge effects make PDIV and repetitive PDIV trends inconsistent across switching frequencies and slew rates, and that WBG switching ringing displaces the spectral zero-magnitude point into the tens of GHz — leaving MHz-band PD invisible even with conventional 300–500 MHz high-pass filtering. Their remedy is signal-side: active cancellation of common-mode voltage and ground current via analog or digital active EMI filters restores PD signal-to-noise ratio without new sensing hardware.

Dawed *et al.* [4] address interturn short-circuit (ITSC) faults in six-phase induction machines using vector space decomposition (VSD): fault-induced odd-order harmonics appear in the $xy$ subspace, which is decoupled from rotor parameters and carries no flux or torque contribution in sinusoidally wound machines. Their multi-LMS compensator adaptively suppresses these harmonics while promoting the first-order compensation voltage magnitude itself — $F_{idx} = \sqrt{(v_{x'}^{(1)})^2 + (v_{y'}^{(1)})^2}$ — as a direct fault-severity index, eliminating post-processing filters, spectral analysis, and dedicated sensors. A plane-fit correction $F^{asy}_{idx} \approx \alpha_0 + \alpha_1 N_m + \alpha_2 T_e$ removes operating-point-dependent asymmetry without lookup tables. Hardware validation on a 1.25 kW SPIM detects two shorted turns ($\gamma = 1.52\%$) at 400 r/min at 17n arithmetic operations per iteration — substantially below multi-PI alternatives. The subspace parity structure (even-order harmonics in $xy$, odd in $\alpha\beta$) additionally confers intrinsic immunity to eccentricity and broken-rotor-bar faults in symmetrical machines.

The shared trend across [2] and [4] is *instrumentation-free diagnostics*: both derive health indices from signals the power stage already produces, at a cost compatible with embedded controllers.

### C. Communication and Aggregation of DERs

Guaiana *et al.* [3] supply the connective tissue between grid operator and DER fleet. Their three-device architecture — a DG/ESS concentrator (Linux SBC + ST8500 PLC modem) at the MV/LV substation, PLC interface bridges at each plant, and an intelligent electronic device (IED) virtualizing heterogeneous converters behind a canonical 16-register Modbus map — is validated both in laboratory and on an LV line of the Favignana distribution network. Measured performance is production-grade: 100% delivery success on PRIME 1.4 channels 3 and 8 (mean latency 0.35–0.81 s), bit error rates near zero for BPSK across all eight PRIME channels, and end-to-end remote command actuation on a 20 kWh storage system in under 4 s — an order of magnitude inside the 50 s requirement of the Italian CEI 0-21 connection rule. Because the design is deliberately hardware-agnostic (any SoC running Python and Node-RED is a drop-in replacement), it constitutes a realistic B2B ingestion boundary; its acknowledged gap is device-level security, deferred to future IEC 62443 alignment.

### D. Electrified Demand and the Smart-Load Edge

Zissis *et al.* [6] frame the demand side. Lighting consumes 13.7% of global electricity and drives 4.2% of greenhouse-gas emissions; solid-state lighting is now decisively dominant (commercial LED efficacies exceeding 210 lm/W plug-in against a 683 lm/W thermodynamic ceiling; white OLED panel efficacy rising from 1 to 160 lm/W between 1987 and 2020, with a projected 325–400 lm/W ceiling). Their argument is less about the sources than about what they enable: a "fourth revolution" in which lighting becomes a networked, sensor-coupled IoT subsystem — the largest distributed actuator in the built environment — raising governance questions (privacy of sensing-rich luminaires, affordability, temporal-light-modulation standards) that fall outside pure power engineering.

### E. Synthesis

Read together, [1]–[6] form a layered but *disconnected* stack: generation-side economics ([1], [5]), conversion-health observability ([2], [4]), a standard-conformant telemetry pipe ([3]), and a massive flexible load ([6]). Each layer is individually mature; none consumes the others' outputs. The concentrator of [3] moves SoC/SoH/power registers but has no notion of hosting capacity, curtailment attribution, or asset health; the fault index of [4] exists only inside a drive's control loop; the curtailment economics of [1] require per-bus time series that no deployed architecture currently delivers at scale; and the flexible lighting fleet of [6] is absent from the curtailment calculus entirely. Closing that loop — a unified DER telemetry plane whose payloads are exactly the quantities [1] and [4] compute, transported by an architecture like [3], and acted upon by loads like [6] — is the contribution this work develops.

## 2.3 Ingest adapter — concentrator → gateway (Python, runs on any P3-compatible SoC or a platform pod)

```python
# services/ingest/modbus_poller.py
# Attaches VVU to the [3] architecture as a SCADA-class Modbus TCP client.
# Memory layout per [3]: AR (availability register) @ 0x00, then one BLOCK per
# connected plant, each BLOCK_WORDS long + a last-update register.
import json, time, jwt, asyncio, websockets
from pymodbus.client import ModbusTcpClient

IED, ABSENT, BLOCK_WORDS = range(0x10), 0xFF, 17
GW_URL, JWT_KEY = "ws://gateway:8080/upstream", __import__("os").environ["VVU_JWT_KEY"]

def word32(hi, lo): return ((hi & 0xFFFF) << 16) | (lo & 0xFFFF)

def read_plant(c, idx):
    r = c.read_holding_registers(1 + idx * BLOCK_WORDS, BLOCK_WORDS, slave=1)
    if r.isError(): return None
    w = r.registers + [0] * (BLOCK_WORDS - len(r.registers))
    pv_present  = w[IED.PV_NOM_MSW]  != ABSENT          # 0xFF sentinel per [3]
    ess_present = w[IED.ESS_NOM_MSW] != ABSENT
    return {
        "id": f"{idx:02d}",
        "pv":  None if not pv_present else
               {"mode": w[0], "powerW": word32(w[1], w[2]),
                "nominalW": word32(w[9], w[10])},
        "ess": None if not ess_present else
               {"mode": w[5], "invMode": w[6], "powerW": word32(w[7], w[8]),
                "soc": w[3], "soh": w[4],
                "nominalW": word32(w[11], w[12]), "nominalWh": word32(w[13], w[14])},
        "contactor": w[15] & 1,
        "lastUpdateMs": w[16] * 1000,
    }

def discover(c):                       # AR bit-scan → live plant blocks
    ar = c.read_holding_registers(0, 1, slave=1)
    return [i for i in range(16) if not ar.isError() and (ar.registers[0] >> i) & 1]

async def run(feeder, bus_map, poll_hz=1.0):
    token = jwt.encode({"sub": "ingest-01", "scopes": ["stream:write"]}, JWT_KEY, "HS256")
    async with websockets.connect(f"{GW_URL}?token={token}", ping_interval=20) as ws:
        c = ModbusTcpClient(bus_map["concentrator_ip"], port=502)
        seq, c.connect() = 0, c.connect()
        while True:
            seq += 1
            plants = [p for i in discover(c) if (p := read_plant(c, i))]
            for p in plants: p["feeder"], p["bus"] = feeder, bus_map.get(p["id"], "unknown")
            await ws.send(json.dumps({"type": "der", "seq": seq, "t": int(time.time()*1000),
                                      "src": bus_map["concentrator_id"], "plants": plants}))
            await asyncio.sleep(1.0 / poll_hz)
```

## 2.5 $F_{idx}$ condition stream — publish path

Two options; prefer (a) for B2B:

- **(a) IED map extension** (vendor registers 0x10–0x17: $F_{idx}$ as u32 fixed-point ×1e6, $N_m$, $T_e$, quality flags) — flows through the entire [3] stack unchanged, including PLC. Requires cooperation from the drive/IED vendor.
- **(b) Out-of-band** from the drive host PC (P4's own serial-acquisition precedent) directly to `/upstream` — works today with unmodified [3] hardware, bypasses PLC.

**Alarm policy (important):** alarm on the *plane-fit residual* $F^{itsc}_{idx} = F_{idx} - (\alpha_0 + \alpha_1 N_m + \alpha_2 T_e)$ exceeding k·σ of the asset's own residual distribution — never an absolute $F_{idx}$ threshold. This is P4's core design philosophy (operating-point-dependent healthy threshold) carried into the platform. If the drive publishes only raw $F_{idx}$ + $(N_m, T_e)$, the server applies the per-asset α vector — which is why `alpha` is optional in `DriveHealthFrame`.

## 3.2 Reference implementation (runnable + self-testing)

```python
# python/vvu_der_reference.py
"""Portable reference implementations + dataset loaders for the VVU DER extraction.
Sources — IEEE Open J. Ind. Appl., vol. 7, 2026:
  [1] Issa 10.1109/OJIA.2025.3627886 · [2] Lee 10.1109/OJIA.2025.3648282
  [4] Dawed 10.1109/OJIA.2025.3647228 · [5] Gerber 10.1109/OJIA.2025.3636406
Self-test:  python vvu_der_reference.py   (numpy only)"""
import csv, math, os
import numpy as np

DATA = os.path.join(os.path.dirname(os.path.abspath(__file__)), "..", "data")

# ── [1] volt-var & curtailment analytics ─────────────────────────────────────
def volt_var_q(v, k_abs, k_gen, v_l, v_u, v_min, v_max):
    """Eq. (1) [1]: Q(V̄) — piecewise volt-var. Sign: Q>0 injection, Q<0 absorption.
    Saturates outside [v_min, v_max]."""
    if v <= v_min: return k_abs * (v_l - v_min)
    if v >= v_max: return k_gen * (v_u - v_max)
    if v < v_l:    return k_abs * (v_l - v)
    if v > v_u:    return k_gen * (v_u - v)
    return 0.0

def eff(p, pmpp):
    """Eq. (9) [1]: generation performance factor = ∫P dt / ∫Pmpp dt ∈ (0, 1]."""
    return float(np.trapz(p) / np.trapz(pmpp))

def voltage_deviation_index(voltages):
    """Eq. (10) [1]: M_f = ΣΣ ||V_n^i|−1| / (N·N_n). Input flattened over
    (timesteps × nodes × phases)."""
    return float(np.mean(np.abs(np.abs(np.asarray(voltages)) - 1.0)))

def pmpp_per_bus(p_nom_loads, p_mult, irrad, sp_pct):
    """Eq. (7) [1]: zero-net-energy PV allocation at bus k for penetration S_P [%]."""
    return float(np.sum(p_nom_loads) * np.trapz(p_mult) / np.trapz(irrad) * sp_pct / 100.0)

# ── [2] PD / WBG-drive insulation ────────────────────────────────────────────
def resonance_freq(l_eq, c_eq):
    """Eq. (2) [2]: f_res = 1/(2π√(L_eq·C_eq)) [Hz] — governs V_peak migration."""
    return 1.0 / (2.0 * math.pi * math.sqrt(l_eq * c_eq))

def v_steady_turn(k, n_turns, v_dc):
    """Eq. (1) [2]: V_steady,k/N = V_dc(1−(k−1)/N). PD-location test: V_res > V_steady."""
    return v_dc * (1.0 - (k - 1) / n_turns)

def pdiv_darkin(t_film, eps_r, rectangular=True):
    """Eqs. (3)/(4) [2]: Darkin PDIV [V]. t_film = insulation film thickness, ε_r = rel. permittivity.
    Rectangular wire ⇒ 2^0.46 ≈ 1.375× higher PDIV (PD less likely) — per [2] §V-B."""
    x = (2.0 * t_film / eps_r) if rectangular else (t_film / eps_r)
    return math.sqrt(2.0) * 163.0 * x ** 0.46

# ── [4] multi-LMS ITSC diagnostics ───────────────────────────────────────────
class MultiLMS:
    """Multi-harmonic LMS compensator, Eqs. (1)–(12) [4]. Tracks each harmonic order n of
    the xy-subspace current i_xy; the per-harmonic estimate v_x/y doubles as fault-index
    source. Weights → [A_n·sinφ_n, A_n·cosφ_n] (Eq. 7)."""
    def __init__(self, orders, mu=1e-3, fs=1.0, f0=50.0):
        self.orders, self.mu, self.fs, self.f0 = list(orders), mu, fs, f0
        self.k = 0
        self.w = {n: np.zeros(2) for n in self.orders}
    def step(self, ixy):
        self.k += 1
        t = (self.k - 1) / self.fs
        out = {}
        for n in self.orders:
            x = np.array([math.cos(2*math.pi*n*self.f0*t),          # Eq. (8)
                          math.sin(2*math.pi*n*self.f0*t)])
            e = ixy - (self.w[n] @ x)                               # Eq. (1): ε = d − y, d = i_xy
            self.w[n] += 2.0 * self.mu * e * x                      # Eqs. (3)/(10)/(11)
            out[n] = float(self.w[n] @ x)                           # Eq. (9)
        return out

class RotaryFaultIndex:
    """Eqs. (20)–(23) [4]: rotate the xy error at −ω_e ([D] = e^{−jω_e t}) — the 1st harmonic
    becomes DC — and track it with per-axis unit-basis LMS (the G^(0) unit). F_idx = |v|."""
    def __init__(self, mu=5e-3, fs=1.0):
        self.mu, self.fs, self.k, self.w = mu, fs, 0, np.zeros(2)
    def step(self, ixy_complex, we):
        self.k += 1
        r = ixy_complex * np.exp(-1j * we * (self.k - 1) / self.fs)  # Eq. (20)
        x = np.array([r.real, r.imag])
        self.w += 2.0 * self.mu * (x - self.w)                       # DC tracking
        return math.hypot(*self.w)                                   # Eq. (23)

def f_idx_corrected(f_idx, nm, te, alpha):
    """Eqs. (24)/(25) [4]: F_idx^itsc = F_idx − (α0 + α1·N_m + α2·T_e)."""
    return f_idx - (alpha[0] + alpha[1]*nm + alpha[2]*te)

# ── [5] SS-WTS stability criteria ────────────────────────────────────────────
def inertia_ratio(jt, gr, j_sc_coil, j_sg, j_sc_pm):
    """Eq. (18) [5]: κ = (J_t/GR² + J_SCcoil)/(J_SG + J_SCpm). All high-speed side; GR=1 direct-drive."""
    return (jt / gr**2 + j_sc_coil) / (j_sg + j_sc_pm)

def accel_coeff(tt, jt):
    """Eq. (19) [5]: α_c = T_t/J_t [rad/s²]. Criteria tables validated at α_c = 0.031 rad/s²."""
    return tt / jt

SLIPS  = [0.5,0.75,1.0,1.25,1.5,1.75,2.0,2.25,2.5,3.0,3.5,4.0,4.5,5.0,5.5,6.0,6.5,7.0]
KAPPAS = [5,10,15,20,25,30,40,50]
_FILES = {("PMSG","xs"):"p5_table4_max_xs_pmsg.csv", ("WRSG","xs"):"p5_table5_max_xs_wrsg.csv",
          ("PMSG","T"):"p5_table6_min_pullout_pmsg.csv", ("WRSG","T"):"p5_table7_min_pullout_wrsg.csv"}
_CACHE = {}

def _table(machine, kind):
    key = (machine, kind)
    if key not in _CACHE:
        with open(os.path.join(DATA, _FILES[key])) as f:
            _CACHE[key] = np.array([[float(x) for x in r] for r in list(csv.reader(f))[1:]])
    return _CACHE[key]

def _bilinear(M, slip, kappa):
    s = min(max(slip, SLIPS[0]), SLIPS[-1]); k = min(max(kappa, KAPPAS[0]), KAPPAS[-1])
    si = min(np.searchsorted(SLIPS, s) - 1, len(SLIPS)-2); ki = min(np.searchsorted(KAPPAS, k)-1, 6)
    s0,s1,k0,k1 = SLIPS[si],SLIPS[si+1],KAPPAS[ki],KAPPAS[ki+1]
    ts = (s-s0)/(s1-s0) if s1>s0 else 0.0; tk = (k-k0)/(k1-k0) if k1>k0 else 0.0
    return float(M[si,ki]*(1-ts)*(1-tk) + M[si,ki+1]*(1-ts)*tk
               + M[si+1,ki]*ts*(1-tk)     + M[si+1,ki+1]*ts*tk)

def max_sync_reactance(slip_pct, kappa, machine="PMSG"):
    """Tables 4/5 [5]: max allowable X_s (p.u.) under symmetrical 3-φ LVRT.
    Salient machines: pass max(X_d, X_q). Below-surface ⇒ stable. Clamped at grid edges."""
    return _bilinear(_table(machine, "xs"), slip_pct, kappa)
```
