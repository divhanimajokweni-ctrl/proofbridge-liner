Core doctrine
The fundamental rule
Never sell to a dysfunctional off-taker
Your only clients are private entities who are desperate for reliable water and have the ability to pay. Municipality never touches your water or your money.
The positioning rule
Industrial water security — not water rights
You are a reliability and cost-predictability provider. This is a product the state cannot deliver. No syndicate competes for what is not a government contract.
Non-negotiable exclusions — where syndicates live
Municipal tenders
Do not respond to open municipal tenders for infrastructure or operations unless you control the entire process and the client is a ring-fenced entity with a clean track record. Payment will be irregular; facilitation fees will be expected.
Government bulk-water PPPs
Do not rely on government bulk-water purchases or PPP structures where you depend on a politically controlled off-taker. The build-operate-sell-to-municipality model is a trap — you will be expected to operate while waiting indefinitely for payment.
Municipal water resale
Do not position as a middleman reselling municipal water. That puts you in direct competition with political water mafias who sabotage infrastructure to sell tankers. You will be targeted. Your source must be independent.
State DFI funding with political strings
Do not seek funding from state development finance institutions unless you have an unshakeable, relationship-based safe path. Debt from a political bank comes with non-financial conditions. Prioritise impact investors, IFC, or European DFIs.
The instead
Customer-funded · contract-backed · physically independent
Your business must derive revenue entirely from private entities under long-term off-take agreements, using your own licensed source and distribution infrastructure. Revenue rings are sealed from municipal accounts.

Legal and financial armour — structure before you take a cent
SPV per asset cluster
Each asset group or contract cluster lives in a separate special-purpose vehicle. If one is attacked legally or physically, the others are insulated. Assets sit in a holding trust or offshore entity with independent operating companies.
Water-use licence
Secure via DWS regional office — not local municipal officials. Complete, compliant application through technocratic consultants. A lawful water use is protected unless revoked through a formal, publicly-scrutinised process.
Ring-fenced revenue
Prepaid smart meters for bulk supply. Direct debit mandates from commercial clients. Third-party payment platforms. Money never touches a municipal account at any point in the chain.
International arbitration
All major contracts include international arbitration clauses. This makes the dispute resolution process entirely outside local political reach.
Community social licence
Reserve 5% of production for a free community standpipe operated by a women's cooperative with your remote-controlled metered valve. Offer a small ownership stake to a community trust with annually-elected trustees, overseen by a reputable NGO. The community then has a direct financial interest in protecting your infrastructure.
Anchor DFI investor
Minority stake from IFC, a European DFI, or an impact fund. Brings diplomatic pressure and media spotlight that syndicates actively avoid. Unlocks blended finance for capex.
Independent board
Include an independent, credentialed board member from the outset. This signals probity to customers, investors, and any regulator. Creates an audit trail that is a forensic liability for anyone attempting extortion.

Sprint R1 — 12-week execution plan · Gqeberha anchor
01
Asset audit — map what you control
List every water source (borehole yield, abstraction licence status), storage capacity, treatment equipment, land in strategic locations, fleet, technology, and relationships with water-hungry businesses. This is your inventory of leverage.
02
Identify the desperate buyer
Walk into any Coega factory, estate manager, or hotel GM. Ask: "What is your monthly cost of water including tankering and production downtime?" You will find someone paying more than R50/kl in real terms. That is your market. One conversation, one number.
03
Draft the water purchase agreement
One page. Fixed tariff at R25–35/kl. 10-year term. 99.5% uptime guarantee. International arbitration clause. Offer a 30-day free trial supply via temporary tanker or IBC totes to prove the model before any capex commitment.
04
Structure the SPV and independent board
Incorporate the operating entity as an SPV under a holding trust. Appoint one independent board member with a clean public profile. This signals probity to investors and creates legal armour before revenue flows.
05
Secure water-use licence — DWS regional channel
Engage a technical consultant who routes through the DWS regional office, not local municipal officials. Submit a complete, compliant application. Parallel-track the environmental authorisation. Never leave a licence as a single point of political attack.
06
Deploy capex and establish social licence
Fund the containerised RO plant (~R1.5–2.5M for 100kl/day) via the anchor client loan recovered through a discounted tariff, or an impact investor taking a long-term SPV dividend. Simultaneously: establish the community standpipe cooperative and ring-fence 5% of output. This is your political insurance, not charity.
07
Prove, replicate, build the private grid
Once the anchor client is live and the model is proven at 99.5% uptime, approach neighbouring properties and extend the pipeline. Each new client improves unit economics. You are now building a private water grid that has never touched a municipal account.
The positioning principle
"Industrial water security" — not a replacement for government water
This framing is legally clean, commercially compelling, and politically untouchable. You are building a utility that works, one contract at a time. That is the most powerful proof-of-model statement you can make.
